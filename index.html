The weekend of the wedding

The weekend of the 25th–27th is La Mercè weekend, celebrating the Patron Saint of Barcelona with massive street festivities. There are multiple traditional Catalan events, activities and concerts that we would love to share with you.

You don’t really need to plan for them and will most likely run into them if you’re walking downtown. We will coordinate which events we attend on Saturday afternoon and Sunday as the date nears.

Some opinions about Barcelona

Moving around Barcelona

The most convenient way to move around the city is by metro or on foot. The metro will run 24/7 through La Mercè weekend, including overnight.

Getting to and from the airport

Aerobús is easy: A1 serves Terminal 1 and A2 serves Terminal 2, and both run 24/7 to Plaça Catalunya. FREENOW (an app for local taxis) and, recently, Uber work in Barcelona after a rather tense history with the taxi guild. Feel free to go down that news rabbit hole.

Safety

While Barcelona is extremely safe in terms of violent crime, pickpocketing is extremely common. Be very cautious: do not keep valuables in an outer backpack pocket or leave anything unattended. A fanny pack is a solution. Yes, this can happen to you. Multiple people attending the wedding have already been pickpocketed in Barcelona, despite having travelled all over the world without incident. Barcelona is currently SOTA in PickPocketBench.

Where to eat & drink

Please don’t eat in “Las Ramblas.”

1881 / La Terrassa de les Indianes: Go for a drink and the port view; that’s really why we like it. Plaça de Pau Vila, 3, Barceloneta.

La Cova Fumada: Tiny, old school and no fuss. Go for tapas and a bomba. Carrer del Baluard, 56, Barceloneta.

Pepa Tomate: Multiple locations, a lunch special and traditional dishes. Ask for the “menu of the day,” not just the “menu.”

La Pallaresa: A classic for churros and very thick hot chocolate. Carrer de Petritxol, 11, Gòtic.

4 Gats: A historic Modernista place where Picasso used to hang around. We mostly like it for the room and the history.

7 Portes: A cool space with traditional food.

Can Paixano (El Xampanyet): A busy tapas bar, extremely crowded—it’s good and cheap—but very nice.

Bosc de les Fades: A completely ridiculous fairy-forest bar next to the Wax Museum. Very touristy, but fun at least once. Passatge de la Banca, 7, Gòtic.

Bar Marsella: Old, scruffy and famous for absinthe. It feels like old Barcelona. Carrer de Sant Pau, 65, Raval.

Mercat de Santa Caterina: A grocery market with produce, tapas bars and prepared food to go.

Dr. Stravinsky, Bestiari, Bareto Olímpic and Boadas: A list of bars we like.

Compartir: On the fancy side—around €70 per person—and needs a reservation on weekends. It’s the tapas restaurant of a renowned Michelin-starred team.

La Central del Raval: A bookshop with a hidden courtyard café. A nice place to stop if you’re wandering around Raval. Carrer d’Elisabets, 6, Raval.

El Nacional: A beautiful food hall just off Passeig de Gràcia. An easy place to pop in for tapas or a drink. Passeig de Gràcia, 24 Bis, Eixample.

Some food we miss when we are abroad

Tapas of many sorts: There are many little nibbles to try, and you’ll try most of them at the wedding. Truita de patates / tortilla de patatas is potato omelette; you’ll see it everywhere. Some are amazing and some are sad. There are many types of cured meat—pernil / jamón is the most famous and fuet is the one we miss most when abroad—and many kinds of seafood, such as chocos (fried cuttlefish), pop / pulpo (octopus) and boquerones / seitons (anchovies pickled in vinegar). They’re usually accompanied by pa amb tomàquet.

Paella & fideuà: Rice or pasta with seafood. For either one, we’d go somewhere that actually does rice and seafood rather than ordering one at random on La Rambla. Arròs negre, black from cuttlefish ink, is our favourite variation.

Something sweet: Crema catalana, mel i mató, ensaïmada (the original version is not vegetarian, but most are nowadays—ask the bakery), flan, and chocolate with churros. The chocolate should be thick enough to eat with a spoon.
